## Priority reading — the three that should decide the project

### 1. Xu, Zhao & Hu (2026) — the one that answers your original question

**The effects of acute and chronic exercise on executive functions and core symptoms in adults with ADHD: A systematic review and meta-analysis**
*Psychology of Sport and Exercise*, 84, 103088.
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1469029226000282
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41638541/
DOI: 10.1016/j.psychsport.2026.103088 · PROSPERO CRD42024595524

**Access: [PARTIAL] — PAYWALLED.** Abstract, Highlights and full Introduction
read. Results, statistics and Limitations not accessible.
**Try the CSUDH library for full access — this is the single most relevant paper
to your thesis and we have not seen its Results section.**

**Why it matters to you.** It pooled acute exercise (inhibitory control
Hedges' g = 0.55, 95% CI 0.32–0.79). For chronic exercise it reports:
*"the limited number of studies precluded quantitative synthesis, and the
available evidence yielded mixed and inconclusive results."* Fourteen studies in
the review, eight in the meta-analysis.

This is the paper that says the adult chronic literature is too thin to answer
your question. Read it yourself before accepting that.

### 2. Dinu et al. (2023) — the negative result

**The effects of different exercise approaches on attention deficit hyperactivity disorder in adults: A randomised controlled trial**
*Behavioral Sciences*, 13(2), 129.
https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9952527/
DOI: 10.3390/bs13020129 · PMID 36829357 · ISRCTN39271564

**Access: [VERIFIED] — free full text.**

**Why it matters.** 159 adults (82 ADHD, 77 controls), 10 min cycling vs 10 min
yoga. Their own abstract: *"There were no effects of exercise on attention,
cognitive or motor impulsivity, or movement in those with ADHD."* And:
*"Exercise reduced attention and increased movement in controls."*

The best-powered adult attention trial available, and it is negative. Any
version of your project that claims exercise improves attention in adults has to
answer this paper.

---

### 3. START trial, primary outcomes (2025) — the strongest positive result

**Physical exercise as add-on treatment in adults with ADHD – the START study: a randomized controlled trial**
*Frontiers in Psychiatry*, 16, 1690216.
https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2025.1690216/full
DOI: 10.3389/fpsyt.2025.1690216 · PMID 41244864 · PMCID PMC12614457 · NCT05049239

**Access: [VERIFIED] — free full text, open access.**

**Why it matters.** 12-week supervised exercise, 63 adults. ASRS total between
groups: MD −6.98 (95% CI −12.30 to −1.65), p = 0.012, Cohen's d = −0.93. A large
effect, and the closest thing to support for your original thesis.

Read it critically. The primary outcome is an unblinded self-report scale, 35%
dropped out, and the inattention subscore has **no published between-group
test** — only a within-group change in an arm that knew it was exercising.

## The rest, read in full

### 4. Zhu et al. (2023) — which exercise ranks best, in children

**Comparative effectiveness of various physical exercise interventions on executive functions and related symptoms in children and adolescents with ADHD: A systematic review and network meta-analysis**
*Frontiers in Public Health*, 11, 1133727.
https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10080114/
DOI: 10.3389/fpubh.2023.1133727 · PMID 37033046 · PROSPERO CRD42022365188

**Access: [VERIFIED] — free full text.**

59 studies reviewed, 44 meta-analysed, 1,757 participants. Open-skill activities
rank first for executive function (SUCRA 98.0%, SMD 1.96, 95% CI 1.15–2.77).
Closed-skill ranks first for inattention (SUCRA 96.3%, SMD −1.51, −2.33 to
−0.69). **Exergaming was the only category that did not beat control.**

Caveats to check yourself: I² 79–88%, no study scored above 8/10 on PEDro,
publication bias assessed by eyeballing funnel plots only, and the paper never
names a single cognitive instrument.

### 5. Mehren et al. (2019) — how long the effect actually lasts

**Acute effects of aerobic exercise on executive function and attention in adult patients with ADHD**
*Frontiers in Psychiatry*, 10, 132.
https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6443849/
DOI: 10.3389/fpsyt.2019.00132 · PMID 30971959

**Access: [VERIFIED] — free full text.**

30 min cycling, 20 adults with ADHD. Reaction times improved (congruent d = 0.81,
incongruent d = 0.65). But check the timing yourself: the flanker task began
*"9.8 min"* after the bike stopped (range 7–16 min), and the ~33-minute probe was
negative — *"implicating a limited duration of exercise effects."*

Also note the interference score — the actual executive-control measure — did not
improve and went the wrong way (77 ms → 83 ms).

### 6. Svedell et al. (2023) — where adherence breaks

**Feasibility and tolerability of moderate intensity regular physical exercise as treatment for core symptoms of ADHD: a randomized pilot study**
*Frontiers in Sports and Active Living*, 5, 1133256.
https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10225649/
DOI: 10.3389/fspor.2023.1133256 · PMID 37255729 · NCT05049239

**Access: [VERIFIED] — free full text.**

n = 14, analysed at n = 5 per arm. Underpowered by design. The value is the
adherence data: 33% dropped out *before attending a single session*, none after
starting. Participant quote: *"It has not been possible to exercise on my own at
all... Even though I felt motivated, it didn't work."*

### 7. Lim et al. (2019) — the best digital attention-training trial

**A randomized controlled trial of a brain-computer interface based attention training program for ADHD**
*PLoS ONE*, 14(5), e0216225.
https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6528992/
DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0216225 · PMID 31112554 · NCT01344044

**Access: [VERIFIED] — free full text.**

172 children, properly randomised. Clinician-rated effect 1.6 points
(95% CI 0.3–2.9). Watch the pattern: effect grows as the rater becomes less
blinded — clinician 1.6, unblinded parent 2.2, parent-rated *internalizing*
symptoms 3.4. No sham control. Zero objective outcome measures.

### 8. START protocol (2023) — where the abandoned arm is documented

**START – physical exercise and person-centred cognitive skills training as treatment for adult ADHD: protocol for a randomized controlled trial**
*BMC Psychiatry*, 23(1), 697.
https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12888-023-05181-1
DOI: 10.1186/s12888-023-05181-1 · PMID 37749523 · PMCID PMC10521407

**Access: [VERIFIED] — free full text, open access.**

Read this next to paper #3. The protocol registers **three** arms including
occupational-therapist-led cognitive skills training for *"time management
skills, planning and organization."* The delivered trial had **two**. That gap is
the most interesting thing we found.

### 9. START body awareness results (2026)

**Physical exercise and body awareness/movement quality in adults with ADHD: Results from the START randomized controlled trial**
*Complementary Therapies in Medicine*, 98, 103372.
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0965229926000555
DOI: 10.1016/j.ctim.2026.103372 · PMID 41903823

**Access: [VERIFIED] — free full text, open access (CC BY).**

Contains **no attention or executive function outcomes at all.** Included because
it documents the 35% dropout and uses one-tailed tests, which is worth noticing.

## Suggested order

1. **Dinu et al. (#2)** — the negative result. Shortest route to understanding why the original thesis is in trouble.
2. **START primary outcomes (#3)** — the positive result. Judge for yourself whether an unblinded self-report scale with 35% dropout convinces you.
3. **Journal of Global Health (unread, top of the table above)** — the one that could change everything. Nobody has read it.
4. **START protocol (#8)**, sections on the three-arm design — the documented gap.
5. Everything else as needed.

## How to read a trial critically

For each paper, answer these before accepting any number:

- Who was studied? Adults or children? Diagnosed how?
- Acute (one session) or chronic (multi-week)?
- What was the control condition? Nothing, or something active?
- Was anyone blinded?
- What exact instrument measured "attention"? Is it named?
- **When** was the outcome measured relative to the intervention?
- Which results were null? Nulls are usually reported quietly or not at all.
- What do the authors themselves list as limitations?
